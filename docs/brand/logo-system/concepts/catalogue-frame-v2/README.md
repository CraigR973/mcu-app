# Catalogue-frame prototypes — round 2

Two image-led developments of the catalogue-frame mark. These are large/display concept studies, not final vector or production app-icon assets.

> Archived exploration: Craig subsequently clarified that the intended direction was the three-overlapping-case mark, developed in `../poster-stack-v2/`. Keep this grid study for reference, but do not treat it as the selected catalogue concept.

## Variants

- `catalogue-frame-actual-posters-v1.png` uses recognisable crops from six authentic film posters.
- `catalogue-frame-concept-art-v1.png` uses six original, text-free genre illustrations in a unified mid-century screen-print language.
- `catalogue-frame-comparison.png` compares both against the flat round-one baseline.
- `catalogue-frame-small-size-test.png` shows direct 128, 64 and 32 px reductions.

## Poster mapping

Reading left-to-right, top-to-bottom:

1. *Jaws* (1975)
2. *An American Werewolf in London* (1981)
3. *Double Indemnity* (1944)
4. *The 39 Steps* (1935)
5. *Casablanca* (1942)
6. *North by Northwest* (1959)

The selection is provisional and intended to test the system. Individual titles can be replaced without changing the core frame.

The authentic-poster composition is an internal prototype. Retaining poster artwork in a publicly distributed production identity would require a separate rights and licensing decision.

## Design findings

- At 128 px both image-led variants retain the catalogue structure and enough subject detail to feel cinematic.
- At 64 px they continue to read as a collection, but individual films are no longer reliably identifiable.
- At 32 px the imagery becomes colour texture. The flat geometric catalogue frame should therefore remain the companion micro mark.
- The concept-art version has stronger palette unity; the authentic-poster version has greater emotional recognition and personal specificity.

## Image-generation brief

Both variants preserve the same near-black field, champagne-gold film frame, four perforations, six-cell grid, burgundy restraint and M-forming diagonal masks. The authentic variant was prompted to preserve poster crops and likenesses; the concept-art variant replaced them with original, text-free shark, werewolf, noir, British suspense, wartime-romance and chase scenes.
